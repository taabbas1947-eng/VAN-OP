# AP26012 — packing a finished lot, and the lot that was never made
**26 August 2026 · three rounds of independent review · approved**

## The first problem: you could not pack a finished lot

**You were right about the effect and wrong about the cause, and it matters which.**

The batch had made 2,020 Kg of a planned 2,710. One lot of 1,010 was certified FIT
by the lab. The bar at the top of your screen said **Ready to pack 1,010** — and the
only buttons were Log shift output and Close batch.

You believed the recent updates had caused it. **They had not.** I ran that exact
batch through the system as it stood before every change made this month — before
the authorisation work, before Supply Chain, before the HG26025 fix, before the
Production conversion, before the yield work. **Seven versions. Every one behaved
the same way: no Pack button.** Nothing broke it. It was never offered.

### Why

A batch is given **one status, decided by its worst part**.

- Produced 2,020 of 2,710 → the batch is *"still producing"*.
- Even at the full 2,710, one uncertified lot → *"waiting QC"*.

Neither of those offers a Pack button. Meanwhile the bar is calculated a completely
different way — lot by lot, straight from the certified quantities — which is why it
told you the truth while the buttons refused to act on it.

### What I changed, and what I deliberately did not

**The packing engine was already correct.** It accepts a batch if *any* lot is
approved, and it caps the quantity at exactly the certified amount. The button was
the only thing missing.

So the change adds a question the screen never asked — *"is there anything on this
batch that can be packed right now?"* — and offers **Pack** wherever the answer is
yes, **beside** the batch's own button rather than instead of it. A batch that is
still producing and holds a certified lot is now in both lists, because it genuinely
is in both states.

**The status rule itself is untouched.** It drives the step dots, the reconcile list
and several counts, and changing it is where this would have broken something.

The quantity rules are the same code as before, byte for byte. Uncertified, disposed,
already-packed, voided and pool material still cannot be packed, and a part-certified
lot is still capped at the certified part. The reviewer packed 13 different batch
shapes to confirm it.

Three more real batches appear in Ready to pack as a result — **VU26164, NP26005 and
HG26022** — all in the same position: still producing, fully certified, nothing packed.
Nothing was removed from the list.

## The second problem: two lots when one was made

Both 1,010 Kg, same shift, same incharge, same date. **One shift was logged twice.**

Lot numbers are minted by counting the lots already there, so the repeat came out as
`-L2` and looked like a second lot rather than the same one again. Nothing objected —
there was no duplicate check of any kind. And nothing could undo it: the only removal
in the system sat in a screen that is never drawn, and was COO-only anyway.

### Two changes

**1. It now warns.** Log the same batch, date, shift and incharge twice and the first
press writes **nothing** and tells you what is already there. Press again and it goes
through — because a batch can genuinely run twice under one incharge in a day.
Change the shift or the incharge and it asks again rather than riding on the last
confirmation.

**2. There is now a Remove control on each lot** of the batch passport. It removes the
lot, the produced total, the shift entry, the production-log row and — on a PO batch —
the order line, in one action, with a written reason, into the corrections register
with your name.

It works from the **lot's own identity**, not its size, which is why it can tell
AP26012's two identical 1,010 Kg lots apart. The old removal matched on quantity and
would have had to guess.

## Two things for you to decide, not just be told

### 1. Who removes a shift's output

You asked for *"Production Manager and above"*. That role does not exist yet, so it
starts with the **Plant Manager** — the same person who reopens a closed batch —
and the COO. **Deliberately not the floor**: the floor logs the output, someone above
them unwinds it.

The day you create the Production Manager role, moving it across is one tick in the
Authorisation panel.

> **Are you content for the Plant Manager to hold it in the meantime, or do you want
> to create the Production Manager role first?**

### 2. What happens to material that fails

A lot the lab has **certified**, **part-signed**, or failed as **UNFIT** cannot be
un-logged. The system sends you to the real decision instead: accept the deviation,
send it for rework, or dispose of it. Only a lot the lab has not touched can be
removed.

The UNFIT case is the sharp one. The Plant Manager holds both the removal right *and*
the deviation and rework decisions — so without this, the person who must decide what
to do about failed material could quietly delete it instead.

> **Are you content that failed material always needs a decision rather than a
> deletion?** I recommend yes. It is the difference between a record that says what
> happened and one that says nothing did.

## One thing to know

Closing a batch and reopening it are still meant to be two different people, and the
system does not stop you giving one role both. That stays your judgement, not the
app's rule.

## Repairing AP26012

> **Correction, same afternoon.** The lab certified L2 by mistake after this went

> written — a certified lot has no Remove. The repair is now Supersede → Remove; the
> exact clicks, and what was built to make them possible, are in
> `AP26012-CERTIFIED-2026-08-26.md`. Do not pack from AP26012 until it reads 1,010.


1. Open the batch passport → **Lots** tab.
2. On **AP26012-L2** (the Pending one), press **Remove**.
3. Pick **Duplicate entry** and say what happened.
4. Produced goes 2,020 → 1,010, and all 1,010 becomes ready to pack.
5. Then press **Pack 1,010 Kg cleared** on the batch.

L1 is certified, so it has no Remove control — which is the guard working.
