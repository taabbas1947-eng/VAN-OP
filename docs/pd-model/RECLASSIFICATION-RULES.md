# PD — re-classification: how records get refiled without anyone being corrected

_Ruled by Tahir, 20 August 2026. This is a rules file, not advice. If code and this
document disagree, this document wins until Tahir signs a change._

> **"Best possible design and manner which makes people feel respected, even when
> corrected. Don't ask reason or justification. But yes — content is editable, the
> record of what it was is not. Live the principle in spirit and acts and flow: the
> correction is the teaching."** — Tahir, 20 Aug 2026

---

## 1. The principle

A model with nine object types will be got wrong for months. That is not a training
failure; **it is what learning looks like.** So the system is built on one sentence:

> **Nobody is corrected. A record is refiled.**

The person was not wrong. They wrote down something true, in the only vocabulary they
had at the time. Someone later put it under a better heading. That is a filing act,
not a judgement, and the whole design follows from refusing to confuse the two.

**The practical stake:** if being refiled feels like being marked wrong, people stop
entering things. An empty system is worse than a mis-sorted one, because a mis-sorted
record can be moved and a record that was never written cannot be recovered.

---

## 2. The rule about reasons — settled

**No reason, justification or explanation is ever required to refile a record.** Not
for a small fix. Not for crossing object types. Not for a merge, a split or a
re-parenting. There is no mandatory comment box, and nothing nags for one.

**A free note is available and always optional.** It is never demanded, never
highlighted as missing, and its absence is never shown.

### But the "why" is still recorded — automatically

The "why" that goes into the record is **not the corrector's prose. It is the
model's own definition.**

When a record moves from Observation to Claim, the system writes:

> *Recorded as a Claim — a Claim is an assertion with an owner and an honest grade,
> which anyone may challenge.*

Nobody typed that. It comes from the glossary the model already publishes. It is
accurate, it is identical every time, and it teaches the reader what the type means.

**This is the resolution of the tension.** Demanding a written reason makes people
avoid small fixes and makes the refiled person feel judged. Recording the definition
costs the mover nothing and teaches everyone who reads it later.

---

## 3. Nothing can be entered wrongly in the first place

The cheapest correction is the one never needed. `MODEL.md` §5 already says the intake
door needs *"one minute, no need to classify."* This makes it structural:

- **Anything entered from the door is `unsorted` by default.** Not "idea", not
  "other" — a first-class state meaning *not yet filed*.
- **`unsorted` is not a backlog of mistakes.** It is the normal front of the system.
- Classification is a **later act by whoever knows the model best**, never a gate on
  someone writing something down.

**If a person cannot choose a type, a person cannot choose wrongly.** Most
re-classification disappears at the source.

---

## 4. What is recorded, permanently

Content is editable. **The record of what it was is not.** Every refiling keeps, and
never overwrites:

| Kept | Detail |
|---|---|
| What it was | Object type, and the field values as filed |
| Who filed it | The original author. **Permanently.** |
| When | Date filed |
| What it became | New object type, new parent, new stage |
| Who moved it | The mover |
| When | Date moved |
| Why | **The model's definition of the new type**, written automatically |
| Note | Optional, free text, never required |

Additional standing rules:

- **The number never changes.** An item keeps its identity through any conversion,
  merge or re-parenting — consistent with killed and parked things keeping their
  number forever.
- **Never overwrite in place.** `MODEL.md` §6 already demands versioning for claims.
  Same rule for type and parentage.
- **Content that does not fit the new type is carried, not dropped.** If a field has
  no home after conversion, it is preserved as a carried-over note on the record. The
  system never silently deletes something a person wrote.

---

## 5. Authorship never transfers

**The original author remains the author.** Refiling changes the heading, not the
ownership of the thought.

- The record continues to read *"from Maleeha"* after a Registrar moves it.
- The mover is recorded as the mover, secondary, in the history — never in place of
  the author.
- If a refiled record later leads somewhere, **the credit belongs to whoever wrote it
  down**, whatever it was called that day.

This single rule does more for whether people keep contributing than any wording
choice below.

---

## 6. The words the system uses

Language is where respect is either real or absent. These are binding.

**Never used, anywhere in the interface, in a notification, or in a log:**

`wrong` · `incorrect` · `invalid` · `error` · `mistake` · `misfiled` · `bad entry` ·
`fixed` · `corrected by` · `should have been` · `please note for next time`

**Used instead:**

| Say | Not |
|---|---|
| "Moved to Bet" | "Corrected to Bet" |
| "Now recorded as a Claim" | "Was wrongly filed as an Observation" |
| "Refiled under Problem P-04" | "Assigned to the correct problem" |
| "Your entry became Bet B-014" | "Your submission was fixed" |

**The framing is continuity, not repair.** The thing the person wrote still exists,
still theirs, now easier for others to find. Nothing was salvaged; something was
shelved properly.

---

## 7. The correction is the teaching — in flow, not in a lecture

When a record is refiled, the author sees **one message, once**:

> **Your entry is now Bet B-014.**
> A Bet is a specific approach taken because we believe something, carrying the one
> result that would kill it.
> — moved by Aisha, 20 Aug · [open it]

That is the whole notification. Three parts and nothing else:

1. **What it became**, with a live link — the record is still theirs to open.
2. **The one-line definition** of the new type — this is the teaching, and it is the
   same sentence every time so it becomes familiar rather than corrective.
3. **Who moved it**, so the person has someone to ask.

**What the message must never contain:** advice for next time, a count of previous
moves, a link to training material, an apology, or anything shaped like feedback.

**The author can answer back.** One action on that message: *"I meant something
else"* — which returns it to the mover as a question, not a complaint. **Correction
that cannot be answered is authority, not teaching.** The ability to reply is what
makes it a conversation between colleagues.

---

## 8. What must never exist

These would destroy everything above, and each is the sort of thing that gets added
later by someone reasonable:

1. **No per-person error count.** Not on a dashboard, not in a report, not derivable
   from the audit log by any screen the system offers. The moment "who files things
   wrongly" is countable, it will be counted, and the honesty of the record ends.
2. **No accuracy score, no quality rating on contributions.**
3. **No approval queue for ordinary entries.** Refiling happens after the fact, never
   as a gate in front of a person trying to write something down.
4. **No public list of unsorted items with names attached.** The `unsorted` state is
   the system's work, not a person's outstanding homework.
5. **No "your entry needed correction" digest.** Ever.

---

## 9. The system learns too, and it learns from patterns not people

The signal in re-classification is real, but it lives at the **type-pair** level, never
the person level.

- If Observation → Claim happens twenty times, **the intake form is asking the wrong
  question**, or the two words are too close in ordinary speech. Fix the form or the
  wording.
- If everything arrives `unsorted` and stays there, **triage has no owner.**
- If one object type is never chosen at entry and always arrives by conversion,
  **that type may not belong in the vocabulary people use** — which is a signal about
  the model, not about the team.

**Aggregate by type-pair. Never by person.** The report reads *"Observation → Claim,
20 times this month"*, and the action is to change a form, not to speak to anybody.

---

## 10. What a refiler can do

Available to the configurable moderator group — Custodian, Registrar and COO to start,
granted like any other PD surface, not hard-coded to a role.

| Action | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Convert type** | Any object to any other. Content moves; nothing is retyped. |
| **Move a value** | From the wrong field to the right one, including across types. |
| **Re-parent** | Wrong Problem, wrong Bet, wrong owner, wrong stage. |
| **Merge** | Two records that are the same thing. Both histories are kept; the merged-away number still resolves and points at the survivor. |
| **Split** | One record holding two questions becomes two, each naming the original as its source. |
| **Bulk** | The learning period will produce dozens at once. Same rules, **one summary notification per author**, never thirty separate ones. |
| **Undo** | Any refiling is reversible. The undo is itself recorded, and needs no reason either. |

---

## 11. Why this is worth the effort

The record only has value if people write things down while they are still unsure
what they have. A person who is unsure and expects to be marked wrong writes nothing,
and the thing that would have mattered in eighteen months is never captured.

So the design goal is not tidy data. **It is that a person who filed something under
the wrong name feels, afterwards, that they helped** — because they did. Someone else
moved a label. The thought was theirs, it is still theirs, and now it can be found.
